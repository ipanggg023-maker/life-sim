# Whole-game grill (2026-11-05)

The user asked to grill every part of the game in depth, one part at a time, in the order a life happens.
Grill all 14 parts first, then write one spec, then build. UI look stays the same (ease of use only).

Parts: 1 birth · 2 childhood and school · 3 how a year is told · 4 people · 5 family and old age · 6 power · 7 fighting and danger ·
8 careers · 9 money · 10 society and kingdoms · 11 dragons and creatures · 12 mysteries and legends · 13 death and the next life · 14 ease of use

## 1. Birth and the starting character (done)
- Birth stays **fully random**. Rerolls stay limited by soul perks.
- **Rare races** (dragon-born, demon-born) unlock through the soul with a deed tied to that race (dragon-born: bond with a dragon or hatch an egg;
  demon-born: make a pact or survive the church's hunt). Unlocking makes that race **roll more often** (e.g. 0.6% → 5%); it's never picked.
- **Stars:** a vague hint at birth ("the midwife says the baby's grip is unusually strong"); the exact count is still revealed at the reading at 6.
- **Birth weight: medium.** A good birth opens doors early; a poor, low-star life can still reach the top by a harder road. Roads for low stars (all four):
  - mastery through years: each 15 years of steady training lifts the rank cap by one, up to master (4); ranks 5–6 still need 8 and 10 stars
  - gear and relics carry a weak body or no magic further
  - risky shortcuts: pacts, forbidden rituals, dragon blood, which cost lifespan, sanity or standing
  - mind over power: dangerous scenes (dragons, wars, duels, creditors) get a wits choice that uses smarts and allies, with its own success %
- **Parents shape childhood strongly:** their jobs, feuds and debts drive childhood events and open early paths.
- **All 8 races get their own life path:** a rite of passage, race-only events across life, how others treat you, a race-only legend.
- **Prejudice is real but beatable:** suspicion in some kingdoms (harder jobs, higher prices, guard trouble) that fame and deeds win over, person by person.
- **Half-races** (half-elf, half-orc, ...) blend both parents' traits and lifespans, with events about belonging to two peoples.
- **Talents:** fewer (2–3), stronger, and each can evolve or awaken from what you do.
- **Family types that can roll:** orphan or foundling (real parents may turn up), adopted by another race, noble or royal blood (hidden or known), outcast family.
- **Birth omens:** war, comet, dragon-flight night, plague year; each tilts childhood events and how people see you.
- **Twins** can roll with linked fate; older siblings shape you (rival, protector, heir to the family trade instead of you).

## 2. Childhood and school (done)
- **Ages 0–5:** short, but each year has one small moment that plants a seed (first word, a fear, a toy, a stranger). Seeds are **hidden**; when one
  returns later in life, the text names the childhood moment it came from.
- **More kinds of school:** scholars' academy, merchants' guild school, bards' and artists' college, thieves' den, and a temple novitiate
  (your god shapes blessings, duties and later church politics), on top of the five schools now.
- **No school is a real path:** apprentice to a craftsman, street kids, a hermit, the family farm, each with its own story.
- **Graduation opens doors by class rank:** top students get offers (guild, court, knighthood, tower); expelled students carry a mark.
- **A school year has a few key beats:** one exam or trial, one personal story (friend, teacher, secret), one study-vs-live choice; class rank moves from these.
- **The school rival is a lifelong thread:** careers, wars, love, old age; it can become friendship, marriage or a death feud.
- **Teens (13–19):** first love (age-appropriate, nothing sexual), rebellion and identity, first real danger, and a clear coming-of-age path choice.
- **One mentor** takes a special interest; what they teach and how you treat them echoes for life, and they may need you later.
- **Child danger:** illness and accidents very rare; real danger comes from risky choices.
- **Race rites at each race's own coming of age:** e.g. orcs 13 (first hunt), humans 16, dwarves 30 (first forging), elves 50 (under the old trees).
- **Long-lived races grow up somewhat slower** (adulthood ~30–50 for elves and dragon-born), with extra youth years, without dragging.

## 3. How a year is told (done)
- **One main story a year**, as now. Small moments stay decided by personality, but **once a year you can redo one** for −3 happiness.
- **"Live quietly for N years":** fast-forward 5 or 10 calm years with a summary. Stops early on any main story with danger, a big family moment
  (birth, death, proposal, a child leaving), health under 40 or new debt, or a returning consequence.
- **Auto-decide stays** and can run several years with fast-forward, with the same stops.
- **Some main stories become multi-year arcs**, 1–3 at a time, shown in a small "threads" list.
- **Many more main stories, tied to routes and races**, so two different lives rarely share them.
- **The narrator notices personality shifts** (crueler, kinder, bolder), and people react to who you've become.
- **Telling:** year titles that read like chapters ("The year the forge burned"); the log grouped into life-stage volumes with a summary each;
  turning points highlighted.
- **Year summary:** the 3 biggest changes, tap for the full list.
- **Failed choices get one line of why**, built from the odds factors.
- **One world news line a year** (a ruler dies, a dragon is seen), things that may touch your life later.
- **Text stays short and vivid** (2–3 sentences a beat).
- **Repetition:** the same small moment can't return within 15 years, and never twice with the same person.
- **Remove the "risk:" lines under choices.** Choices show only the label and success %.

## 4. People and relationships (done)
- **Romance in stages:** meeting → courting → together → engaged → married, each with its own scenes and tests. Adults only, nothing sexual.
  Love interests can say no or leave, and want: matching values, time and attention, support for their own dream, and safety.
- **Marriage friction:** family approval on both sides (status, race, old feuds); the lifespan gap for mixed-race couples; politics for nobles and rulers
  (alliance marriages, love matches cost standing).
- **People have visible lives:** close people get jobs, marry, have children, fall ill, rise or fall; some events ask you to help.
- **A close circle of ~12** gets stories and memories; others fade to acquaintances you can reconnect with.
- **Feelings as words** (the one or two strongest), all 8 numbers on tap. **Memories:** a person's top 3 about you, in their words.
- **Friendships are earned and tested** (loyalty vs. gain, a secret, a debt); best friends can become sworn siblings.
- **Enemies make moves over years:** rumours, sabotage, hired thugs, hitting your family. You can make peace, outwit or destroy them.
- **Betrayal happens, with warning signs** (rising jealousy, secrets, debts), never random.
- **Meeting people comes from where you live and what you do** (job, school, guild, tavern, travel, city), plus a "go out and meet people" activity.
- **Grief lasts and changes you:** a few years of lower happiness by closeness; how you grieve (drink, work, faith, revenge) shapes personality; anniversaries return.
- **The party is a real crew:** classes, personal quests, loyalty that can break, members can die, a shared party name and fame.
- **Household staff for rich lives:** steward (money), bodyguard (safety), servant (gossip and secrets), each with loyalty.
- **Keeping in touch:** letters and visits, and a yearly note about who you've been neglecting.
- **New person actions:** give a gift (fitting their traits and dream counts more), ask a favour (spend what they owe or their loyalty),
  confront or insult, introduce people (matchmake, connect, broker peace).
- **Famous people notice you as your fame and standing rise** (summons, duels, alliances, rivalries); low-fame lives only hear of them.
- **People actions share the yearly time budget with activities**, so busy years leave less time for people.

## 5. Family and old age (done; builds on Release 4)
- **Grandchildren are real people:** they join the family list, can be taught, and can be chosen as heir if a generation is skipped.
- **Raising children:** a yearly parenting style (strict, warm, free, absent) that shapes the child and what they think of you; kids grow talents
  and dreams of their own that can clash with yours; sibling rivalry for favour and the inheritance; adoption (orphan, ward, apprentice).
- **Having children:** decided with your partner; pregnancy and birth get a short scene; some years it doesn't happen; races differ in how often.
- **Ageing is gradual, with choices:** strength and memory fade with clear signs; you choose how to meet it (healers, potions, rest, denial).
- **A last scene you can shape** when death from age is near: who's at your side, last words, a last wish (forgive, give away, tell a secret).
  It echoes in the heir's life.
- **Family reputation across lives:** crest, motto, family trade, old enemies; heirs start with the family's name, good or bad.
- **More callings:** elder of the town, last great work, atonement, one last adventure (on top of the five built).
- **Inheritance disputes:** passed-over siblings can contest the will; the heir chooses to share, buy them off, go to court or cut them off.
- **Family secrets surface across generations:** a hidden sibling, a parent's crime, a stolen inheritance, a curse on the bloodline.
- **Divorce with children:** their feelings shift with how it went; some stay with the other parent and drift from you.
- **Long-lived races outliving descendants is its own thread:** each loss a scene; keep the line going or withdraw from mortals.
- **Remarriage and step-families**, with friction and messier inheritance.
- **Where you live in old age is a choice:** alone, with a child, at a temple, or in your estate with staff.
- **Adult children can join the family trade** while you live and inherit it already running.

## 6. Power (done)
- **Training plus real tests:** yearly training builds points, but each breakthrough needs a trial scene (duel, monster, meditation crisis) that can fail.
  Failing is a setback (lose points or get hurt, wait a year or two), never death, unless you pick a reckless trial on purpose.
- **Techniques are learned, used and named:** from a teacher, a scroll or a fight; they appear as choices in fight scenes; at master rank you create
  your own named technique.
- **Spells are choices in scenes** (fire in a siege, illusion in a heist), costing that year's mana; learned from books, teachers and ruins.
- **Spellblades** (dual path) get their own arts but train both tracks slower.
- **Corruption:** forbidden arts (death magic, pacts, blood rites) give fast power but build corruption: personality darkens, people fear you,
  the church hunts you, and at high corruption you may lose control.
- **Gear with history:** weapons gain kills, famous fights and names, grow stronger, and become heirlooms with their own legend.
- **Seek out masters:** famous masters in the world test you before teaching; each teaches different techniques; leaving one has consequences.
- **Craft and lore ranks (0–6)** for smithing, alchemy, healing and lore, grown by work and study.
- **Power tab:** rank, what the next breakthrough needs, and plain comparisons ("you could beat a bandit captain, not a dragon").
- **Age:** body power peaks in prime years and slowly declines; magic keeps deepening.
- **Peak power (ranks 5–6, ascension) is world-changing:** kingdoms court or fear you, dragons treat you as an equal, ascension is a true ending.
- **Elements** decide which spells come easy or hard, how some people and creatures react, and lean your personality a little.
- **Seclusion:** leave 1–5 years to train hard; big gains, but people drift, job and money suffer, and the world moves on.
- **A few meaningful consumables:** rare potions (healing, strength, a second chance), bought, brewed or found, used as choices in danger.
- **Each weapon style has a clear edge and weakness** that scenes check (bow at range vs. close quarters, shield survives but kills slowly,
  daggers for stealth jobs).
- **Rename ranks and titles across the game so they fit.** The user's request: many names don't suit what they describe. Examples: body ranks
  are all sword words (นักดาบ, ยอดนักดาบ, เทพดาบ) even for axe, bow or fist users; guild ranks go iron → copper (reads as a step down);
  "นักบุญดาบ" sounds religious. Draft a full list (body and magic ranks, guild tiers, job titles, other titles) in the spec for approval first.

## 7. Fighting and danger (done)
- **Rule for every danger activity: it goes deep and continues while you succeed.** (The user's words: each thing goes deep, e.g. a dungeon lets you
  keep going if you succeed.) A dungeon run continues floor by floor and on into deeper dungeons; a contract chain leads to bigger clients;
  a bounty leads to the gang behind it; a hunt leads to the beast's lair; each step raises reward and danger, and you can stop and keep what you have.
- **Big fights play in 2–3 beats** (opening, a turn where you use a technique, spell, potion, ally or wits, an ending). Small fights stay one choice.
- **Death is rare and earned:** most failures injure; death comes from pushing on when warned, fighting far above your level, or war.
- **Lasting injuries change your life:** a lost eye (worse with bows), a bad leg (slower), a burned hand (no fine crafts); healed by rare magic or lived with.
- **Recovery:** rest a year, pay a healer, or use healing magic or a potion; untreated wounds can worsen into lasting injuries.
- **Danger also comes to you:** monsters at your town or road, bandits and crime, assassins for famous lives, nature (storms, avalanches, plague, famine).
- **Dungeons are short expeditions** in one sitting: each floor is push on, rest or retreat; loot grows and danger rises.
- **A bestiary with weaknesses** (fire, silver, spears, stealth) and homes; knowing a weakness raises your odds; it fills in as you meet monsters.
- **War: choose your role** (soldier, officer, healer, spy, supplier, deserter, draft-dodger), each with its own war story; outcomes change the world.
- **A contract board of 3 named jobs a year** with clients and rewards; some have twists; clients remember you.
- **A full underworld route:** thieves' guild ranks, heists, smuggling, a price on your head, guards, prison and escapes.
- **Prison is its own stretch of life:** allies, enemies, escape plans, bribes, learning from prisoners.
- **Killing has consequences:** outside war or a sanctioned duel, guards, a trial, the victim's family's revenge, and a personality shift;
  self-defence can be argued.
- **Fleeing is usually possible, at a cost** (loot, fame, someone you protect); ambushes, sieges and a dragon that saw you can't be fled.
- **Bounty hunting with moral twists:** innocent targets, bribes to let them go, friends who come after you.
- **Mounted and sea fights get their own scenes** (mount bond, ship and crew matter).
- **Nerve:** bold lives fight better outnumbered, cautious ones flee and survive better; a bad defeat leaves fear until you face it again.
- **A named nemesis beast** can cross a life several times, growing over the years.
- **Defending your city:** fight on the walls, lead the militia, evacuate family, bribe, or flee; your home and estates depend on the outcome.
- **Poison, curses and plague are slow threats** that worsen over a few years unless cured.
- **Loot:** mostly gold, sometimes a named find (weapon, map, monster part, a sealed letter that starts a story).
- **Gear breaks only in dramatic moments**, then is reforged stronger or lost. No upkeep chores.
- **A peaceful life can avoid fighting:** forced danger always has a non-fighting way through (hide, pay, talk, flee).

## 8. Careers (done; builds on Release 2)
- **New job families:** court and law (scribe, judge, diplomat, tax collector, royal adviser), faith (priest, inquisitor, temple healer, high priest),
  arts (bard, painter, playwright, jester), sea and travel (sailor, ship captain, explorer, cartographer, caravan guide).
- **Each job has its own story arc:** signature scenes by level (first commission, first oath, first caravan) and a job-only crisis.
- **Your own business grows step by step:** a small shop → staff → new cities, rivals and bad years; it continues while it succeeds and can pass to the heir.
- **One job plus one side gig** (contracts, busking, tutoring, smuggling) sharing the yearly time.
- **Real workplace politics:** coworkers compete for promotions, form cliques, steal credit or cover for you; a boss can be loyal, corrupt or about
  to fall, and you can take their place.
- **Leaving leaves a mark:** fired for cause follows you; quitting well keeps a reference; people remember how you left.
- **A guild for each trade** with ranks, dues, rules and politics; membership opens jobs and protects you; breaking rules gets you expelled.
- **A body of work:** a smith's swords, a bard's songs, a builder's bridges get names and spread, bringing clients, rivals and legends.
- **Apprentices from level 3**, who can help, surpass you, steal your secrets or carry your name.
- **A mid-life turning point (35–50):** stay or change course; changing costs rank and money, related skills carry over.
- **Ask for a raise once a year**, with risk.
- **Hiring is a short trial that fits the job** (forge a blade, answer the magistrate, spar the captain); contacts and school rank help.
- **Patrons and households:** serve a noble, mage or hero; rise and fall with them and get dragged into their feuds.

## 9. Money (done; builds on Release 1)
- **Trade with goods, routes and prices:** buy cheap in one city, sell high in another; prices move with war, seasons and events; a route continues while it pays.
- **Investing in ventures and people:** a friend's shop, a voyage, an expedition, a mine; pays off big, fails, or turns into a story.
- **Taxes by kingdom:** war years raise them; evade (risky), bribe, or move.
- **Gambling** with a risk of a habit that hurts money, family and personality.
- **Land with people on it:** farms, villages, a mine; tenants have needs; treat them well or squeeze them; grows into a lordship.
- **One gold currency everywhere**; differences show as prices and taxes.
- **Poverty is hard but has its own paths:** begging, odd jobs, kindness of strangers, crime's pull, and ways up.
- **You can be the lender:** loans at interest; borrowers repay, beg, flee or resent you; mercy or collection.
- **Theft if careless:** rich lives with weak security can be robbed; a vault, guards or a loyal steward protect you.
- **Charity builds standing:** fund a temple, orphanage or bridge; a legend line; some gifts come back as help.
- **A yearly lifestyle:** frugal (saves, costs happiness and standing), comfortable, lavish (costly, raises happiness and standing, draws flatterers).
- **Booms and busts:** harvests, famines, a gold rush, war embargoes shift prices, wages and trade for a few years.

## 10. Society and kingdoms (done)
- **Each kingdom has its own culture:** values (honour, trade, faith, magic), customs, favoured races and laws that shape which routes thrive there.
  Moving kingdoms is a real choice.
- **A path into court:** commoner → courtier → adviser → lord → ruler through service, marriage, intrigue or force; court scenes with factions, plots and favour.
- **Ruling means yearly decisions** (laws, war, taxes, famine relief, rebels, heirs); kingdom stats move with them; you can be overthrown.
- **A living history:** kingdoms rise, fall, merge and split over centuries; past lives' deeds shape the world each new life sees.
- **Several reputations** (honour, cruelty, wealth, skill, faith), spreading city by city; different people care about different ones.
- **Factions:** knightly orders (oaths, quests, codes), mage circles (secrets, forbidden knowledge, tower rivalries),
  secret societies (hidden goals, betrayals, world plots), rebels (overthrow a ruler, free a people).
- **Several faiths** with gods, temples and politics; blessings and duties; churches hunt heretics and demon-born; holy wars can happen.
- **Class is rigid but climbable** (wealth, deeds, titles, marriage); it shapes who talks to you and which jobs and marriages are open; nobles sneer at new money.
- **Travel is a journey with events:** time, money, road or sea events; a new kingdom brings new laws, culture, people and fresh standing.
- **Courts and trials** for crimes, disputes and debts: bribe, argue, call witnesses, or flee; harsh-court kingdoms punish harder.

## 11. Dragons and creatures (done)
- **Raising a dragon is a life-long bond:** egg to great dragon over decades (feeding, training, its own personality and wishes, growth stages);
  it outlives you and remembers your family. After you die it **stays if your heir is bonded**, otherwise it leaves and may return in a later life
  as friend or foe.
- **Ancient dragons are powers of their own:** they hold territory, make deals, wage war and take sides; befriending, serving or slaying one changes the region.
- **Dragons can be wiped out:** each slain dragon is gone for good; too many deaths weaken magic and break old pacts, and the world remembers who did it.
- **Dragon-born have kinship with obligations:** easier bonds, demands from dragon clans, rival dragons, and human fear.
- **New creatures:** mounts (horses, griffons, giant wolves, sea serpents), rare magical beasts to tame (phoenix, unicorn, basilisk),
  spirits and fey (forest spirits, fey bargains, house spirits), undead and demons (ghosts, liches, demon lords).
- **Pets get their own moments,** age and die (with grief), and some grow (an owl becomes a familiar).
- **Beast markets:** legal for mounts, pets and monster parts; dragon eggs only on the black market, with dangerous buyers, hunters and dragon revenge.
- **Wise beasts and fey talk and bargain** (riddles, deals that come back with a twist).
- **A full beast-tamer route:** beast handlers' school → stable → menagerie → royal beast master, taming rarer creatures as you rise.
- **A creature map by region** (griffons in the peaks, serpents on the coast, fey in old forests); overhunting empties a region.
- **Simple bloodlines** for mounts and some beasts; famous bloodlines bring buyers and fame.
- **Mistreated creatures grow wild:** refuse, run away, attack; a mistreated dragon may come back for revenge.
- **Flying mounts** make travel faster and safer, reach sky islands and peaks, but draw attention.
- **Creatures join big fights in the turn beat** (a dragon's fire, a wolf's ambush), risking their life for a bigger chance.

## 12. Mysteries and legends (done)
- **Legendary items (the user's request):** rename all of them, add more legendary weapons, **add Excalibur**, make every one truly usable
  (it appears as a choice in fight scenes and changes what you can do, not just a bonus), and give **each weapon its own special events** that only it has.
  Excalibur is public-domain legend; write our own story for it in this world.
- **Relics have will and history:** they choose their wielder (combat, wit or heart tests), carry the history of past holders (including your
  past lives), and have a price or curse.
- **Forge your own relic** as a life's work: rare materials, high craft rank, a great sacrifice; it enters the world with your name.
- **Mysteries are real clues you piece together** in a journal; some scenes ask you to pick the conclusion; wrong guesses lead to false trails;
  solved mysteries change the world.
- **Sagas: fewer, bigger, route-tied.** Each route and race gets 1–2 epic sagas over many years, ending in a world legend.
- **Seeds of fate return often enough to feel:** about a third come back within 5–20 years, some much later or in the next generation.
- **Hidden wonders:** lost cities, sunken temples, sky islands, the world tree, found through clues, maps and travel; some only one life per world can claim.
- **Prophecies that can be wrong:** signs may point to you or a rival; fulfil, defy or fake them.
- **Your legend is retold and changes** over centuries; future lives hear songs about you, sometimes wrong, and can set the record straight.
- **Rare world crises** (demon invasion, dying sun, a god's death) span several lives; your lives decide the outcome.
- **A hidden history of the world** (why dragons fade, where magic comes from, the first kingdom), uncovered in pieces across lives.
- **A soul journal across lives** for clues, lore, places and legends (in-world, your heirs keep the notes).
- **Gods act rarely and with a price:** answer great prayers, send omens, choose champions, punish; gods have rivalries.
- **Curses** on you or your line with a clear effect; breaking one is a quest, maybe across generations.
- **Other realms are rare journeys with their own rules** (fey, underworld, sky; time runs differently, food traps you), great rewards, real risk.
- **Ghosts of past heroes, including your past lives,** rarely appear to warn, guide or ask for something left undone.

## 13. Death and the next life (done)
- **Rewinding fate stays rare and scarring:** one mercy before 30, plus rare threads; each rewind leaves a scar and shifts the world a little.
- **The soul carries:** faint memories (a skill comes easier, a place feels familiar, an old enemy's descendant feels wrong); soul ranks
  (old soul, ancient soul) with small permanent perks; unfinished business (a vow, a revenge, a love) that follows you as a story hook.
- **The soul shop adds unlocks earned by deeds** (rare races rolling more often, new birth omens, family types, starting kingdoms), plus the current items.
- **The death screen tells your life as a story card** (volumes, turning points, people who mattered, your legend), then clear continue previews.
- **Continuing as a stranger: choose soon (5–20 years, people you knew are still around) or far (50–200 years, your old life is legend).**
- **Recognition is rare:** your old spouse, child or rival may feel something familiar; a rare scene lets you hint or tell the truth.
- **Route goals across lives:** ladders per route and race (first master smith, first dragon rider, first orc king) that unlock new options.
- **Ways to die have last moments:** sudden deaths get a final act or word; some deaths can be cheated once (a god, a pact, a miracle) at a lasting price.
- **A short afterlife passage:** your soul is judged by deeds, faith and personality, which tilts the next birth a little (an omen, a curse, a guide).
  Birth stays random; the soul never picks where it is reborn.
- **Rare undeath:** death-magic masters or cursed lives may rise as a lich or linger as a ghost, a short strange continuation with its own ending.
- **The graveyard becomes a family tree and a hall of lives** with story cards; visiting a past life's grave in the world gives a small blessing or memory.

## 14. Ease of use (done; look and colours unchanged)
- **Do tab grouped** (work, training, people, adventure, life, money), with a "suggested this year" row of 3 from your goals and urgent needs;
  locked items say what unlocks them.
- **Optional life goals (1–3)** with progress and next steps; achieving one is a milestone.
- **In-game alerts:** someone needs you, danger ahead (debt, health, an enemy's plot), ready to act (breakthrough, promotion, egg close to hatching).
- **Small hints** the first time a system appears; can be turned off.
- **Keep 6 tabs**, sort content inside; each tab opens with a 3–5 line summary, details in folds.
- **Saves:** auto-backup of the last few lives on the device; undo the last age-up only for a mis-tap, right away
  (before any choice in the new year, never for choices or deaths); export a finished life story as text.
- **Settings:** text speed, story pace, hints on/off, larger text.
- **Keep the odds breakdown on tap** (the small i).
- **Phone fixes:** less scrolling, bigger and better-spaced buttons, popups whose choices are visible without scrolling.
- **People tab:** close circle on top (family, partner, best friends, enemies), acquaintances folded; each row shows the strongest feeling in words
  and an alert dot if they need you.

## Rules for the whole game (the user's last requests)
1. **No repeats:** each +1 year should show events you have seen before very rarely, or never.
2. **No two lives share a story:** every life played must have a different story.
3. **Everything is real:** every action affects the character and the world for real, all the way through.
4. **Gender-correct names and titles:** titles, epithets and descriptions must match the character's gender
   (e.g. "นักผจญภัยหนุ่ม" for a woman is wrong). Check every gendered word (หนุ่ม/สาว, พ่อ/แม่-style titles, ท่านชาย/ท่านหญิง, etc.).
